## 2026-10-13: Two walls sharing a few works no longer lock onto one each (#350)

<!-- prawduct: scope=household-schedule-lock | release=v0.5.0 -->

**Why:** Two walls whose themes share only a few works could each show one work
for the whole three-day horizon. Each wall was rebuilt against the other's
published schedule, which could not move; against a wall showing `b` throughout,
the only schedule the household rule allows is `a` throughout, and every roll
answered the other with its mirror image. The owner ruled on 2026-10-13 that a
republish may rebuild other walls' slots to come, vetoing the wave-4e decision
that it never moves a wall nobody touched.

**What changed:**
- `schedule.build` takes `movable` (walls rebuilt after this one, of which only
  the slot covering now counts) and `align_to` (the slot starts of the wall just
  built). A kept slot runs on until the slots after it are in step with those
  boundaries, by under one interval, while the whole slot stays under two and it
  clashes with no wall it was not already clashing with. Without that, two walls
  whose slots start seconds apart could not alternate two works: every slot of
  one would overlap one of each on the other. Not solved, and said in the module
  docstring: intervals that are not multiples, and a republished wall with
  shorter slots than a neighbour sharing only two works (the clash is named).
- `DisplayService._write_v2` rebuilds every wall whose feed shares a work with
  the wall it writes, in wall-id order, each keeping the work on it now. A
  neighbour is rebuilt from its hung theme when its feed carries that theme,
  else from what its feed carries, and never starts fresh. Each is logged by
  name. A wall sharing no work is untouched, to the byte.
- A neighbour is movable only if it holds two works no fixed wall holds
  (`schedule.movable_walls`): with one work, or with its others always shown by
  a wall outside the pass, it has nothing to step aside to, so its slots to
  come count as fixed. A neighbour that cannot be rebuilt
  (a `ServiceError`) keeps its schedule and is named in a warning; the act
  asked for stands. `_republish` is the one place the roll, a setting change
  and a pass decide how a wall republishes keeping its picture. Reconciliation
  reads each wall's feed as it reaches it, so it cannot write a neighbour's
  old schedule back over its rebuild. The `step_display` docstring no longer
  says another wall's schedule is left alone. Mixed paces: #374.
- `Reconciliation.republished` still lists only the walls whose feed changed for
  the works in question.
- Tests: these changed meaning, by the ruling.
  `TestOneFeedPerWall::test_one_walls_rewrite_does_not_touch_another_walls_file`
  hung one theme on both walls and asserted the study's file never changed; it
  is now `..._does_not_touch_a_wall_sharing_no_work_with_it` with disjoint
  themes, and `test_a_wall_sharing_a_work_keeps_the_work_on_it_now` holds the
  sharing case to its slot now. `test_showing_a_work_now_reaches_one_wall_and_not_the_other`
  asserted the study's whole schedule unchanged, which passed only because both
  walls were hung in the same second. It is now `..._and_never_both_at_once`.
  `test_undo_puts_it_back_on_the_walls_whose_theme_holds_it` asserts each wall
  rather than their union, which the lock had forced. Three integration tests
  asserted a second wall hanging the same theme was never rewritten
  (`test_a_step_on_one_wall_leaves_another_where_it_was`,
  `test_a_step_leaves_every_other_walls_feed_alone`, now `..._picture_alone`,
  and `test_a_second_wall_hangs_the_same_theme_without_disturbing_the_first`);
  each now holds that wall's picture (its first slot's work and start) and, in
  the last, its works. `test_from_this_theme_takes_it_out_of_that_theme_and_off_that_wall_now`
  asserted the study carried both works; with the hall showing `stays`
  throughout, the study rebuilt beside it keeps `stays` only as its picture now,
  so it asserts the work its comment names, `gone`. Every new test was seen
  failing against a re-break: no pass, `movable` ignored, no lengthening, each
  lengthening guard removed, the already-clashing allowance removed, a
  neighbour rebuilt from its feed alone, and the theme-must-match guard removed. The `_single_work` fixture first covered five
  hours and could not tell `movable` from its absence; it covers the horizon.

## 2026-10-13: A wall shows only the shapes it is set to (#370)

<!-- prawduct: scope=wall-shape-filter | release=v0.5.0 -->

**Why:** A portrait wall was sent wide landscapes, which it shows as a strip
across the middle of the screen. The owner ruled on 2026-10-13: a min–max
range of width ÷ height on each wall, set by hand and off by default, with the
screen's reported shape shown only as a hint; and a range that leaves nothing
is honoured, so the wall goes dark and says so.

**What changed:**
- `Wall` gains `min_aspect_ratio` (tallest) and `max_aspect_ratio` (widest),
  null for no limit. `programming/shape.py` reads a bound as `W:H`, a decimal
  or `any`, speaks it back as `W:H` (or four significant figures), and counts
  a work inside with 1% of give, measured on its upright master.
- A wall's feed leaves a playable work outside its range off, named with
  reason `outside_wall_shape`, after the curator's keep-off and never over a
  Library refusal. On a wall with nothing hung, the range waits for the next
  hanging. An explicit show-now still puts any work up.
- The build's summary counts each of the curator's reasons apart from the
  works that are not displayable, and ends "so the wall is dark" whenever
  nothing reaches the wall. A published build logs that summary, at WARNING
  only for an unchosen exclusion or a dark wall.
- `set_rotation` widened into `set_playback`: `PUT /api/walls/{id}/playback`,
  where a field left out is left as it is, and `art_display(action='set_playback')`.
  MCP's `not_displayable` key is now `not_on_the_wall`. `WallOut` carries
  `tallest`, `widest` and `shapes`, and `sizes_judged_against` the screen's
  `shape`.
- Walls: a *Shapes it shows* panel (presets, custom bounds, the screen's shape
  as a hint), and a card whose wall is dark says so at the top.

## 2026-10-13: Shuffle and the interval are the wall's, not the theme's (#353)

<!-- prawduct: scope=wall-rotation-settings | release=v0.5.0 -->

**Why:** The owner's ruling 2 of 2026-10-11 said a theme has an order and is
never shuffled. Whether a wall shuffles, and how long each work stays up, are
settings of the wall that plays the theme. On 2026-10-13, with #370 (a wall
filtering its theme by aspect ratio) in view, the owner chose to store both on
the wall itself, beside `mat_mode`, not on the hanging. They survive hanging
another theme and clearing the wall, and #370's filter will go in the same
place.

**What changed:**
- `Wall` gains `rotation_interval_seconds` and `shuffle`, where null means the
  deployment default. `Theme` loses both. `DisplayService.pace(wall)` is the
  single reader for a build, a rebuild and a rolled horizon.
  `DisplayService.set_rotation` republishes the wall's feed at once and keeps
  the slot currently on the wall.
- `migrations.carry_rotation_to_walls` gives each wall the values its hung theme
  had set. It logs, by name, any value whose theme hangs nowhere, then drops
  the theme's columns. A wall's own value is never overwritten, so an
  interrupted open is finished by the next one.
- HTTP: the wall's two settings are set through `PUT /api/walls/{id}/playback`
  (#370, which replaced this branch's `/rotation` before either was released).
  `ThemeOut` loses both fields and `ThemeDetailOut` loses `shuffled`.
- MCP: `art_display(action='set_playback')` (#370's widening of
  `set_rotation`, also never released). This surface drops a null
  argument, so the default is sent as words: `shuffle='default'` and
  `default_interval=true`. `art_theme(action='update')` refuses both settings
  by name. Seven other action descriptions in `art_display`'s menu were
  shortened to keep it under the 2 KB client budget, which a test enforces.
- Client: Walls › *How {wall} is set up* has minutes and an order, set with
  *Set the rotation*. The facts are read back from the published feed. The
  Theme page caption says only *In curated order.*
- Tests: one test changed meaning. `test_the_order_copy_says_whether_position_decides`
  became `test_the_order_copy_says_only_that_the_theme_is_in_order`, because the
  ruling removes that sentence. Two store widening tests now check
  `is_hidden`, since `themes` no longer carries the rotation columns. Every new
  test was seen failing against the old code or a deliberate break (the carry
  left unregistered, the `COALESCE` guard removed, the republish removed,
  shuffle read from the default only, the old client, the not-a-number guard
  removed).
- **Deploying it: copy the catalogue first.** `carry_rotation_to_walls` runs
  the first time the catalogue is opened and drops the theme's rotation
  columns; a value set on a theme that hangs on no wall is logged and then
  gone. Take
  `sqlite3 <art root>/catalogue.sqlite ".backup <backups dir>/pre-wall-playback-<timestamp>.sqlite"`
  before deploying; the way back is that copy and the previous image, together.

## 2026-10-12: Show more keeps the keyboard's place; three intermittent tests made deterministic (#343, #344, #365)

<!-- prawduct: scope=flaky-tests | release=v0.5.0 -->

**Why:** Tests failed intermittently. One, #344, was a client defect that
only looked like a flaky test. Focusing *Show more* scrolls its
row into view, which makes the scroll fetch a page of its own. Pressing the
button then focused the first work of the page after that one, so the works
the scroll brought were behind the keyboard, reachable only with Shift-Tab.
The test failed whenever the scroll's page landed between the focus and the
press. It failed 3 of 4 runs alone and passed when the press came first.
#365 bounded a real MCP round trip at 2 s of wall-clock time, so a busy
machine could fail a correct `start`. #343 compared two reads of an Ask
thread whose cards fetched their pictures from the live Commons site. One
request failed (`ERR_BLOCKED_BY_ORB`), and *Picture unavailable* appeared
whenever the internet answered, so it was in one read and not the other.

**What:**
- *Show more* counts arrived works from when the keyboard reached the button,
  not from the press, and forgets that count when focus leaves.
- `test_the_grid.py` builds the scroll-page-first case deterministically and
  pairs it with a click after the keyboard has left. They fail against the old
  client and against a client without the `blur` reset, respectively.
- The browser suite's `ui` fixture aborts every request to an origin other
  than the suite's server, so no browser test reaches Commons. A test that
  needs a picture to load uses `pictures_load`. That moved one artist-page
  test, which had been passing only when Commons answered. The Ask thread's
  settle wait now waits for every picture box. A new test covers the guard;
  without it the test fails on a machine with a network, while offline
  Chromium fails the request on its own and the test cannot tell.
- The other wall-clock bounds of #365's shape are now derived from the
  quantity they separate from, not from a guess at a round trip:
  `FakeEngine`'s gate is `GATE_SECONDS`, the browser surface's start and
  status and the MCP status checks bound by a half of it or of
  `STATUS_HOLD_SECONDS`, and the look budget test by its own slow fetch. The
  grid tests read `NEAR_THE_END` from `collection.js` rather than copying it.
- `test_a_tile_whose_image_cannot_be_loaded_says_so` waits for its sentence,
  not for `.card-image-absent`, which the catalogue's works with no master
  paint at first paint. The old wait returned before the 404 had been heard,
  and CI failed this PR's first run on it.
- `test_discovery_tool.py` asserts that `start` returns while the fake engine
  holds phase 1 open, with a bound of half the gate's own, not 2 s. It fails
  by name when `start` runs phase 1 inline.

**Deploy:** server only (the browser client).

## 2026-10-09: Programming computes a wall's schedule (wave 4e)

<!-- prawduct: scope=wave-4e-schedule | release=v0.5.0 -->

**Why:** Major 2 replaces the Player's own rotation with a schedule decided
on the server for every wall (`re-architecture.md` § Order of work, wave 4e).
`build-plan-wave-4e-schedule.md` carries the plan and its decisions.

**What:**
- Chunk 01: `programming/schedule.py`, a pure function that builds one
  wall's slots over a three-day horizon. It works against every other wall's
  published slots, so no work is on two walls at once except as a named clash.
  A rebuild keeps the slot on the wall now, and showing a work now or skipping
  starts fresh. Every work comes up once per cycle, and none twice running.
  Nothing calls it yet: chunk 02 publishes it.
- Chunk 02: every path that publishes a wall's major 1 manifest now publishes
  its major 2 feed beside it (`theme-manifest-{wall}.v2.json`), and
  `/walls/{id}/manifest/v2` serves it with its ETag. The facade offers each
  work's current presentation master, with its size read from the file, and
  its mat colour. Re-hanging the same theme keeps the slot on the wall now.
  Another theme, *show now* and *next* start fresh. A withdrawn or refused work
  leaves the feed, and a new mat colour or master replaces its entry without
  moving a slot. A heartbeat with under two days of horizon left rolls the
  horizon forward. A work with no master stays on major 1 and is named. A
  theme with works but no masters publishes no feed, so its Player stays on
  major 1 (`player-contract.md` § The cutover, amended).
- Chunk 03: a wall's mat mode. `walls.mat_mode` (nullable; an older file
  gains the column when it is opened) holds `none`, `proportional` or `full`,
  or nothing for the Player's own choice. Setting it republishes the wall's
  feed with `settings.mat.mode` and moves no slot. `PUT /api/walls/{id}/mat`,
  `art_display(action='set_mat_mode')` (`players_own` clears it) and a *Mat*
  panel in each Walls card's *How {wall} is set up* set it. The panel confirms
  in place and stays open. `mat_mode` is on `WallOut` and the MCP wall fields.
- Chunk 04: too small for this wall. Each screen size a wall's Player reports
  in a minor 2 heartbeat is kept per display (`reported_screens`), and sizes
  older than a week are forgotten. A wall is judged against the largest size
  reported in that week, not the latest. A work is too small when its master,
  never enlarged, fills less than half of the box inside the contract's
  default mat (`programming/adequacy.py`, held to the no-density mat vectors).
  The judgement reads each master's size from the wall's feed, so it asks the
  Library nothing. It informs and takes nothing off the schedule. It is shown
  under the work on the wall on Walls, as a "too small for {walls}" badge in a
  theme member's *Size*, and as `too_small` and `too_small_for` on the HTTP
  and MCP walls and theme reads, beside what the judgement was made against
  (`sizes_judged_against`) or why none was made (`sizes_unjudged`).
  **A heartbeat whose `capabilities` lacks a readable `screen` is now refused**
  (`400`), where it was stored. The contract requires `screen` in
  `capabilities` and Arrt Player always sends it, so a conforming Player is
  unaffected. A Player that sent capabilities without one would see its
  heartbeats refused, and its wall shown as silent, until it does.

**Rolling back:** reverting the merge restores the code. The stored state it
adds is inert to the older code. `walls.mat_mode` and the `reported_screens`
table are read by nothing before this branch, and a reported size is deleted
with its display (`ON DELETE CASCADE`), so removing a client still works. Each wall's
`theme-manifest-{wall}.v2.json` beside its manifest is served by no route, so
every Player falls back to `v1` on the 404. Delete the v2 files to tidy up.
Nothing needs a migration back.

**Tests:** `tests/unit/test_schedule.py` states the contract's slot rules, the
household rule (a clash only where no work of the wall could avoid one, on
fresh and kept rebuilds), the cycle and stability as Hypothesis properties.
Examples pin each case the properties reach only by chance. A mutation sweep
of the module ran 31 mutations, all caught: 30 by a failing test, and one (a
zero-length slot let through) by the suite hanging, because the build never
ends. The first pass let seven survive; each now has a test, or (the
de-duplication of work ids) was replaced by a refusal that is tested.
Chunk 02: `tests/unit/test_major_2_feed.py` drives each publish path in the
plan's table. `tests/feed_guard.py` checks every feed any test builds against
the schema and the root suite's `semantic_errors`, loaded from its file
rather than copied. `test_player_surface.py`'s "major 2 answers 404" became
major 3: wave 4e builds major 2, and the rule it protects (an unbuilt major
answers 404) is kept with the next major up. A sweep of 30 mutations left
five alive. One test had been passing only because a fresh slot and a kept
one shared a second; it now ages the feed first. The other four have tests.
All 30 are now caught. End to end, Arrt Player's own pull and compositor, run
from its own interpreter against a booted server from this branch, asked for
`v2`, adopted the feed, verified and cached the master, and composed it at
1920×1200: black beyond a mat of the work's colour.
Chunk 03: the service, HTTP, MCP and browser tests, and a test that a file
from before the column gains it. `test_a_patched_entry_keeps_the_feeds_settings`
planted `settings` in the file by hand. A feed's settings now come from the
wall's record, so a planted value is overwritten by design. It became
`…keeps_the_walls_settings`, which sets the mode through the service. The

the exact columns of `walls` (`test_catalogue_store.py`, the client and

## 2026-10-09: Wave 4d — compositing moves to the Player

<!-- prawduct: scope=wave-4d-compositing | release=v0.5.0 -->

**Why:** The fourth of wave 4's seven plans (`build-plan-wave-4d-compositing.md`).
A major 2 work arrives as an unmatted presentation master, and until now
nothing on the Player could draw its mat, so the Player read major 2 but never
asked for it. This plan gives it a compositor and the Frame's geometry, then
asks for major 2 first. The owner ruled the same day that until the repository
split two sessions may run, one per plane (`re-architecture.md` § Agents).

**What:**
- **The compositor** (`arrt-player/src/arrt_player/compose.py`): the
  contract's layout, held exactly to every `contract/vectors/mat-geometry.json`
  vector, then the work drawn at that rectangle in a work-shaped mat (or
  `full`, or `none`) on black, never enlarged. A work whose mat colour cannot be
  read is drawn as `none`. A composed file is named by a key over everything
  that moves a pixel.
- **The Player's geometry** (`config.py`): the Player reads `TV_PANEL_*` for
  the Frame and `MAT_*` for every wall, under the server's names, and names
  them in its startup line. **`test_config.py`'s guard "the plane holds no fact
  about the television's size" is replaced by its opposite**, because
  `feeds-and-players.md` ruling 7 moved that fact here.
- **The wall composes** (`programmes/schedule.py`): each work the feed names
  is composed in a thread, one at a time, the slot's work first, and a work
  not composed yet is passed over for the pass. A master that will not decode
  costs its work; a failed write is retried after five minutes. A tidy removes
  compositions no work needs, and keeps the picture on the wall until another
  replaces it. A switch of major calls `Programme.entered()`, so the programme
  switched to puts its own picture up. **The rotation now takes the wall on
  the switching pass**, not at its next interval (an inference, put to the
  owner).
- **Asking for major 2:** `REQUESTED_MAJORS` is `(2, 1)`, and the Frame writes
  `capabilities` with its configured panel. A wall served a lower major asks
  for the higher one about once a minute, not every poll, and at once if the
  major it was served stops answering. **Tests whose assertions changed by

**What:** both sides of the search clause pass through `search_fold`
(casefold, NFKD with the marks dropped, and a short table for letters Unicode
does not decompose, such as ø and œ). The catalogue adapter defines it on its

remembers what it folded, so that a request's repeated evaluations stay cheap.

(`tools/search_latency.py`, which now times the folded clause too). The HTTP
route, the MCP `list` action and the typeahead are tested with an unaccented
query, as are a German ß, Greek, a ligature, and a name stored decomposed.
Watched failing against the unfolded code first: 13 route tests and the
typeahead test, every unaccented or non-ASCII spelling among them.
